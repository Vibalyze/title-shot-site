Privacy policy

Title Shot: MMA Career Sim for iOS and Android · Last updated 11 November 2026 (anonymous statistics, error reports and “Write to us” from version 1.1.1)

Title Shot collects no personal data. It has no accounts and no ads, it never asks who you are, and your career is saved as a small file on your own phone. From version 1.1.1 it sends a few anonymous gameplay statistics so we can see which parts of the game work, and you can switch them off in the game’s settings. If you write to us from the game, we receive your message. Versions up to 1.1.0 send nothing at all.

What the game sends (version 1.1.1 and later)

Anonymous usage statistics. The game sends short gameplay events to our own server, for example “career started”, “camp plan: wrestling, hard”, “fight won by KO in round 2”, “event answered: option 1” or “career ended: Gatekeeper”. Each event holds numbers and fixed labels chosen by the game, plus the app version, the operating system and its version, your phone’s language and time zone offset, and the country of the connection.

  • Events are tied only to a random install ID made on your phone (and a random ID per session). It is not your name, not your Apple or Google account, not a device identifier and not the advertising identifier. A new random ID replaces it after 13 months.
  • The name and nickname you type for your fighter, and anything else you type, are never part of an event.
  • The server derives the country from the connection; it never stores or logs your IP address.
  • Raw events are deleted after 120 days. Daily totals that hold no IDs are kept for 25 months.
  • Offline, events wait on the phone (at most 1,000) and are sent later. The game works fully without a connection.
  • Turn it off any time: tap the gear, then switch off “Share anonymous stats”. Off deletes the events still waiting and nothing more is sent.

Anonymous error reports. If the game hits a software error, it sends an “error” event with the kind of error, a short fingerprint, and the names of the game’s own code files and lines where it happened, so we can find and fix the bug. The error’s message is never sent (it could contain something you typed). These reports go through the same anonymous statistics, with the same install ID, retention and off switch.

Where the install came from (iPhone only). On first launch the game asks Apple’s AdServices framework for a token and sends it to our server once, so we can learn whether the install came from an Apple Search Ads campaign (campaign, ad group and keyword, the country and the date). This is Apple’s privacy-preserving attribution: it contains no advertising identifier and nothing about you, it is not tracking, and the game shows no tracking prompt. If statistics are off, nothing is sent.

“Write to us”. If you send us a message from the game’s settings, we receive the message, its type (bug, idea or other), the app and system details above, the install ID (so we can match a bug to what happened), and your email address only if you choose to give one so we can reply. Messages are kept for 24 months. This works even when statistics are off, because you send it on purpose.

Our server runs on Cloudflare, with the database in the European Union. Cloudflare processes the data for us as a service provider. We do not sell data, share it with advertisers or data brokers, or use it to track you across other apps or websites.

What we never collect

No name, no contacts, no location, no photos, no microphone, no camera, no advertising identifier and no device identifier. The app contains no advertising libraries, no third-party analytics or crash reporting service, no in-app purchase libraries and no social or sign-in integrations. On Android, version 1.1.1 adds the INTERNET permission for the statistics and “Write to us” above, and nothing else uses it.

What the app stores on your device

One file, career.json, inside the app’s own documents folder. It is unusually small, because of how the game is built: the game is deterministic, so instead of saving the state of your career it saves the instructions for rebuilding it, and replays them when you come back.

The whole file is:

  • a seed — one number the game uses to decide what happens
  • the name and nickname you typed for your fighter
  • the background you picked (kickboxer, wrestler, and so on)
  • the list of choices you made, in order
  • two small numbers the game uses to check the file still matches its own version of the writing and the rules

That is all of it. There are no timestamps, no device information and no identifiers. It is plain readable JSON and you can open it with any text editor. The save file is never transmitted.

The name you type is treated as a character name, not as your name. It is never sent anywhere and never shown to anyone else, because there is nobody else in the app.

A second file, hall_of_fame.json, keeps a one-line summary of each career you finish — the fighter’s name and nickname, final record, belts, title defences, age at retirement, the ending and the seed — so the start screen can show your past careers. It stays on the device like the save.

A third, even smaller file, review.json, records only when the game last offered the rating prompt, so that it does not ask too often. It holds dates and counts and nothing about you. Small files in the app’s support folder hold the random install ID, your statistics on/off choice and the events waiting to be sent.

The game’s content — every event, every line of fight commentary — ships inside the app as bundled files. Nothing is downloaded at runtime, so there is no request to a content server and nothing to log.

Device backups

Because the save lives in the app’s documents folder, it is picked up by your phone’s normal backup: Android Auto Backup on Android, and device or iCloud backup on iOS. That is deliberate, so reinstalling the app does not delete a career in progress. Those backups are handled by Apple and Google under their own privacy policies and your own backup settings. We cannot see them, and we have no way to read your save.

The rating prompt

After a big win, the game may ask the operating system to show its standard “rate this app” sheet, at most a few times ever, and never after a loss. That sheet is drawn and run by Apple or Google, not by us. If you leave a rating or review, it goes to the store under the store’s own privacy policy, and we only ever see what the store publishes.

Accounts, online play, leaderboards

There are none. There is no login, no friends list, no chat, no cloud save and no online leaderboard. The other characters in the game — your opponents, your coach, the promoters, the commentators — are invented by the game. They are not other people and they are never presented as other people. The entire game works with the phone in aeroplane mode; the statistics simply wait until a connection is back.

If online features are ever added, this page will be updated before they ship and the store listing’s privacy answers will be redone to match.

Advertising and purchases

The app contains no advertising and no in-app purchases. There is no ad network in the build, so there is no ad profiling, no App Tracking Transparency prompt. Nothing in the game can be bought, and no fight can be paid for.

Deleting your data

Uninstalling Title Shot deletes the app’s folders, and with them the save, the hall of fame, the rating-prompt file, the install ID and any events still waiting. Turning statistics off stops all sending at once. To have the statistics and messages from your install deleted from our server, email us; we will help you find your install and erase it.

Two things to know:

  • Your phone’s backup may still hold a copy after you uninstall. Clearing that is done in your device’s backup settings, not in the game.
  • Starting a new career replaces the existing one. There is one save, and beginning again overwrites it.

Children

Title Shot is intended for players aged 12 and over and is not directed at young children, but little of that matters here: the app collects no personal data from anyone, of any age. There are no ads, no purchases, no chat, no user profiles and no way for one player to see another player’s content. The anonymous statistics identify no one, and nothing a child types into the game leaves the device unless they send it through “Write to us”.

We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If a child sends us a message with an email address, a parent can ask us to delete it. If you believe something in the app contradicts this page, please write to the address below and we will fix it.

Your rights

If you are in the EU/EEA or UK, the GDPR gives you rights to access, correct, export and delete personal data, to object to its use, and to complain to a supervisory authority (in Estonia, the Andmekaitse Inspektsioon). The statistics are anonymous and we cannot link them to you on our own, but if you give us your install’s details or the email you wrote from, we will act on any request. Your save file is yours, on your device, and you can read it with any text editor.

Changes to this policy

If the app starts doing something this page does not describe — ads, purchases, cloud save — this page is updated first, with a new date at the top, and the store privacy forms are updated with it. We will not quietly add collection under an old policy.
